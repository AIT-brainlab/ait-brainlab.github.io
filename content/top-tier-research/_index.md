If you want to become a leading scientist in your field, make publishing at its top venues a long-term goal. Conferences such as ACL, CHI, NeurIPS, ICML, and CVPR are places where researchers share work with peers across natural language processing, human–computer interaction, machine learning, and computer vision. Aim for the venue where the people who best understand your research question will see your work.

## What you gain

A top-tier venue can bring your research to more people in your field. You also learn a lot by preparing a submission. You have to explain why the question matters, show what your work adds to the closest studies, and make a convincing case that your evidence supports your conclusions.

## What you risk

Aiming for a top-tier conference can put your graduation timeline at risk. Rejection may mean revising the paper, running more experiments, or waiting months for the next deadline. If your graduation depends on acceptance, agree with your supervisor early on a realistic submission timeline and a fallback venue.

## How to grow over time

You can start by publishing in other venues that fit your work. Use each paper to ask a sharper question, improve your methods, and learn how peers respond to your ideas. As your work gets stronger, aim for the leading venues in your field. Over time, the research you contribute and the care you take with your evidence are what earn your colleagues’ respect.
