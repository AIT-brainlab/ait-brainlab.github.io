**A paper explains what the evidence allows others to conclude.** Write while reading, building, and testing: a missing link in the argument often reveals the next investigation you need.

## Establish the Argument Before Polishing

The [Research Guide](/researchguide/) teaches the five-part story: **background → problem → methodology → findings → contribution/significance**. Start with one connected sentence per part, then expand only where the reasoning needs support.

For a proposed study, write “findings pending.” Keep expected contributions separate from demonstrated results. For completed work, replace the plan with what actually happened, including uncertainty and failures.

Use one central message to organise related contributions. A paper can contribute several connected things; it should still be clear why they belong together.

## Give Each Section a Job

| Section | What the reader needs to understand |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Which important question remains unresolved, why prior work does not settle it, and what this paper establishes. |
| Related work | How the closest approaches differ, what they already explain, and why the remaining distinction matters. |
| Approach or system | The core idea, design rationale, assumptions, alternatives, and enough detail to reproduce the relevant behaviour. |
| Methodology | How data, comparisons, measures, sampling, and analysis can support the intended claims. |
| Results | What was observed, with denominators, uncertainty, and clear links to the question. |
| Discussion | What the pattern means, plausible competing explanations, implications, and concrete limits on the conclusion. |

Adapt this structure to the contribution and venue. Learn from a close paper's argument and reporting choices; do not copy its prose or inherit its methods without checking their fit.

## Show the Difference Between a Claim and Its Evidence

Suppose a sales-agent study finds more product exclusions correctly mentioned in generated responses.

| Claim | What would support it? |
| --- | --- |
| “The agent mentions exclusions more accurately.” | Independent checks against verified product facts under a defined evaluation protocol. |
| “Customers understand exclusions better.” | Evidence from an appropriate comprehension task; the agent's own rating is insufficient. |
| “The system preserves human agency.” | A defined meaning of agency and evidence of meaningful control or informed choice beyond factual accuracy. |

These are proposed evidence requirements, not results. Avoid moving from the first claim to the third simply because the story sounds persuasive.

## Revise the Reasoning First

**Weak:** “Our novel multi-agent architecture improves trust and customer decisions.”

**More precise, before the study:** “We will compare the proposed disclosure method with a matched baseline. We will measure exclusion comprehension and choices against explicit scenario requirements. Any claim about trust or agency will require separate measures.”

The revision names the comparison and limits the claim. It still needs related work, a justified design, and actual evidence.

For each paragraph, check whether its main point is specific, its evidence is relevant, and its conclusion follows. Replace vague words such as “better,” “effective,” and “robust” with the actual outcome and conditions. Distinguish observed patterns from proposed explanations.

## Make Figures Do Explanatory Work

**Figure 1** should make the problem and central insight understandable. The **methodology figure** should show how conditions, components, data, and outcomes connect. Use matching names in the figures and text; remove components that do not help explain the study.

A polished diagram cannot repair an unsupported claim. Captions should explain what is shown, whether it is a proposal or measured result, and what the reader should notice.

## End a Writing Session with a Research Decision

Ask a reader to identify the main claim, strongest evidence, and weakest inference. Revise the most consequential issue first. If it needs new evidence, return to the study rather than rewriting around the gap.

Update the paper and linked GitHub record after meaningful changes. Keep protocols, code versions, raw results, and detailed logs in linked artifacts so the manuscript remains a coherent account rather than a work diary. See [AI for Research](/ai/) for the setup and save-progress prompts.
