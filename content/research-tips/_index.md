**Make progress by resolving an important uncertainty.** A smaller project with a clear question and convincing evidence can teach us more than a large system with many unexplained features.

## Choose a Question You Can Investigate

Start where three things meet: a problem you care about, something the research community still needs to understand, and work you can realistically carry out with your team.

A topic names an area. A question identifies an uncertainty. A study specifies how evidence could resolve it.

| Step | Illustrative sales-agent example |
| --- | --- |
| Topic | Honest, adaptive sales agents. |
| Uncertainty | Does the position of a limitation disclosure affect what a customer understands? |
| Question | Does presenting the same exclusion before rather than after a recommendation change comprehension and product choice? |
| First investigation | Inspect related disclosure studies and pilot one controlled conversation to check that the task and questions make sense. |

This is a candidate question, not a verified gap. A supervisor can help narrow it; the student must understand and defend the resulting choices.

Before committing, explain **why the answer matters, what the closest work already establishes, what evidence could change your mind, and what resources you need**. Check access to data, participants, expertise, equipment, and time before designing a large study.

A narrow question can support a broader research direction. Do not promise to solve trust, honesty, personalisation, and human agency in one experiment.

## Read to Make a Decision

Begin with three close papers for your first discussion. Select by the question and evidence, using strong relevant venues and specialist work. Three papers start the conversation; they do not establish novelty.

For each paper, record:

- **Claim:** What does it actually establish?
- **Evidence:** Which comparison, data, measures, or analysis support that claim?
- **Boundary:** Under what conditions might the finding not hold?
- **Consequence:** What should change in your own question or study?

Trace **references backward** to foundations and **citations forward** to follow-up work. Search alternative terminology and neighbouring fields so one vocabulary does not hide a close predecessor. Include recent preprints, clearly labelled.

Read the methods and limitations of the closest papers, not only their abstracts. Borrow a measurement, baseline, or study design because it fits your question; explain any changes. Keep authors' findings separate from your interpretation.

Finish with a short synthesis: **“We know …; we still cannot explain or establish …; this matters because …; the comparison that could clarify it is ….”** If the last sentence is missing, more searching may help less than a supervisor discussion or a small diagnostic test.

## Work in Short Evidence Loops

Before adding a feature, ask what uncertainty it will resolve. After an investigation, record what changed in your understanding.

1. **Choose one unresolved issue:** a literature overlap, weak measure, competing explanation, or feasibility risk.
2. **Choose the smallest useful test:** reading a crucial paper, reproducing a baseline, inspecting errors, or piloting a task.
3. **Inspect the outcome:** include failures, unexpected patterns, and inconclusive evidence.
4. **Decide:** continue, simplify, narrow the question, or change the approach with your supervisor.

Agree a time or effort budget. Reading indefinitely and polishing infrastructure indefinitely can both postpone the decision the project needs. Fix routine software bugs directly; they do not each require a scientific experiment.

**Depth is explaining what the evidence means and where it stops.** It can involve careful design, qualitative interpretation, theoretical reasoning, or a well-controlled comparison. It does not require a large model, many experiments, or long working hours.

For your next meeting, bring **one evidence-backed conclusion, one unresolved issue, and your proposed next step**. The [research decision note](/downloads/research-decision-note.md) can hold this record.
