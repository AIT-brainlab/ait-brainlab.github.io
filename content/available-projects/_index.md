These are our current project directions. Students can discuss a focused role within one of these efforts or propose a related idea. The final scope depends on the student's interests, preparation, degree requirements, and the team's resources.

Support acknowledgements below describe project support; they do not imply that a funded student position or scholarship is available. Discuss current opportunities with the supervisor.

## AI and Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)

**Supported by the Gates Foundation.**

We explore how AI can support trustworthy and useful digital public infrastructure.

- **Facial verification and liveness detection:** Verify identity and distinguish a live person from a presentation attack, with careful evaluation of errors and robustness.
- **AI agents for DPI:** Investigate agents that help people navigate DPI services and workflows, with appropriate permissions, verification, and human oversight.
- **Verifiable credential (VC) use cases:** Explore how verifiable credentials can support practical service interactions and how their use should be evaluated.

**Possible starting point:** Agree on one verification task, agent workflow, or credential use case; define the baseline and evaluation criteria before building.

## Traffic Crash Analysis

**Supported by Toyota Mobility Foundation.**

We combine computer vision with traffic-conflict and crash-analysis concepts to understand risky road interactions. A particular interest is **time to collision (TTC)**: an estimate based on the motion assumptions used, rather than a direct measure of crash probability.

- Detect and track road users in video.
- Estimate trajectories and measures such as TTC under clearly stated assumptions.
- Analyse conflicts and crash sequences, including how tracking errors and scene conditions affect the conclusions.

**Possible starting point:** Evaluate one tracking or conflict-measurement component on an agreed video dataset, including its failure cases.

## Sign Language

**Supported by NBTC.**

We develop deep-learning approaches for **sign-to-text** and **text-to-sign**. These tasks require attention to language structure, temporal information, and the usefulness of the output to sign-language users.

- Build and evaluate models for recognising or translating signed sequences into text.
- Investigate representations and generation methods for producing signed output from text.
- Evaluate meaning, intelligibility, and limitations with suitable data and, where feasible, sign-language users.

**Possible starting point:** Reproduce an agreed baseline and analyse its errors on one defined task. The target sign language and available data will be agreed with the team.

## Honest and Adaptive Sales Agents

**Supported by insurance companies.**

We investigate how to build sales agents that are **honest, ethical, effective, adaptive, and capable**. Effectiveness includes helping customers make informed decisions and finding a suitable outcome.

- Ground product explanations in verified information and communicate uncertainty and limitations.
- Adapt explanations and questions to customer needs without deception, pressure, or exploitation of vulnerability.
- Evaluate task completion, factual accuracy, suitability, transparency, and respect for customer choice.

**Possible starting point:** Define a bounded sales conversation and build an evaluation set that tests both useful assistance and behaviour under pressure to mislead.

## Finding Your Role

Master’s students will usually own a focused component within a PhD or grant project. PhD students develop a broader original contribution, which may connect with one of these efforts. Specific roles, data access, resources, supervision, and any funding arrangements are agreed individually.
