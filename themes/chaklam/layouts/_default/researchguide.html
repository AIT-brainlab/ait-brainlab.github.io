WELCOME

Ultimate Guide to Your Research Success

By Chaklam

Research is not just building an application. It is asking a meaningful question, finding convincing evidence, and contributing knowledge that other people find useful.

The research test: What new understanding will other researchers, designers, or practitioners gain from your paper—and why would it matter to them?
HOW TO THINK

From information to wisdom: Bloom’s pyramid

Research is difficult because we must move beyond describing facts to explaining them, judging evidence, and creating useful new knowledge.

HOW TO THINK

Bloom taxonomy in action

Follow each column downward: the question becomes less about recalling facts and more about understanding causes and making better decisions.

Bloom Selling more sushi AI helping people regulate emotions AI-simulated humans
Remember
Information		 How many sushi boxes did I sell last week? What emotions do people report when using an AI support chatbot? What answers do AI-generated survey participants give?
Understand
Information		 Which sushi sells most, and at what times? How does the chatbot respond when users say they feel sad, angry, or anxious? How similar are those answers to responses from real people?
Apply
Knowledge		 Can I use sales patterns to adjust how much sushi I prepare each day? Can I apply established emotion-regulation strategies to the chatbot's responses? Can I use AI-generated responses to test a survey before giving it to real participants?
Analyze
Knowledge		 Why do some customers buy once but not return: price, taste, freshness, or variety? Why does the same AI response calm one person but upset another? On which questions, groups, or situations do AI-generated answers differ most from real people's answers?
Evaluate
Wisdom		 Which change actually increases repeat purchases without reducing quality or profit? When should AI offer advice, ask a question, adapt its tone, or avoid intervening? For which research questions can simulated answers be useful, and when must we ask real people?
Create
Wisdom		 How can I design and test a sushi experience that makes customers return regularly without relying on discounts? How can we design and evaluate AI support that adapts to a person's emotions, values, and culture to help them regulate emotions appropriately? How can we design a research method that uses AI simulations for early exploration but checks important findings with real participants?
THINKING TOOLS

"Critical" thinking tools

Weak starting question: “Can an AI chatbot help students cope with stress?”

Thinking tools ↑ help us climb toward deeper questions and wiser judgment toward wisdom.

Thinking tool How the question changes Why it helps
Challenge assumptions Can chatbot advice sometimes increase stress instead of reducing it? Challenges the belief that more AI help is always better.
First principles What do stressed students actually need before we decide to use a chatbot? Starts from the human need, not the technology.
Systems thinking How do deadlines, sleep, friends, and chatbot use affect stress together? Looks at interacting causes.
Second-order thinking If students rely on a chatbot for months, does their ability to cope independently change? Investigates longer-term effects.
Inversion When might a chatbot make a stressed student feel worse? Identifies risks and failure conditions.
Root-cause analysis Is the main problem a lack of advice, or an overwhelming workload? Investigates the underlying cause.
Thinking by analogy What can chatbot designers learn from how peer-support groups listen? Transfers ideas from another domain.
Compare alternatives When is a chatbot more useful than journaling or talking with a friend? Defines a meaningful comparison.
Stakeholder perspectives Would students, counselors, and instructors define “helpful” in the same way? Reveals competing needs and evaluation criteria.

POSSIBLE ANSWERS

How thinking tools improve the career question

Original question: Can AI help students choose a career?

Thinking tool How the question changes Why it helps
Challenge assumptions Could confident AI recommendations stop students from exploring careers they might enjoy? Tests whether more guidance is always beneficial.
First principles Do students really need a job recommendation, or a better understanding of their interests and values? Starts with the underlying need.
Systems thinking How do family expectations, financial pressure, grades, and AI recommendations jointly shape choices? Looks at interacting influences.
Second-order thinking After using AI career advice repeatedly, do students become more or less confident deciding independently? Examines longer-term consequences.
Inversion When might AI career guidance lead students to regret a choice? Finds failure conditions.
Root-cause analysis Is career uncertainty caused by missing information or by conflicting personal goals? Investigates the underlying cause.
Thinking by analogy Could AI borrow the questioning approach used by good career counselors rather than giving instant answers? Transfers a useful approach.
Compare alternatives Do students make better decisions with AI recommendations, self-reflection questions, or human counseling? Identifies meaningful baselines.
Stakeholder perspectives Do students, parents, and career counselors disagree about what makes a career choice successful? Reveals different definitions of success.

Novelty

New to the field. Interesting to others. Important enough to investigate.

NOVELTY

Novelty is judged by the research community

Novelty is not about what feels new to you. Other researchers decide whether your idea is new relative to published work, interesting to the field, and important enough to change understanding or practice. The only credible starting point is to read closely related papers.

Idea Novel? Why?
First stress chatbot at my university No A new location alone teaches little beyond prior work.
Use a newer LLM for emotion support No A model swap alone is not a research insight.
Discover when AI advice makes people feel less in control Potentially A meaningful effect, if not already established.
Generate 10,000 simulated survey respondents No More simulated responses alone do not establish validity.
Identify which questions AI-simulated people answer differently from real people Potentially Could show when simulations should not be trusted.
Change a dashboard's color No Appearance alone rarely yields new understanding.
Find when AI reminders help beginners but distract experienced students Potentially Could reveal an important boundary condition.

SECTION

Related work

Find out what is known, what is disputed, and what is still unanswered.

RELATED WORK

8 Ways to Synthesize Related Work

Don't just summarize papers. Compare them to discover new research opportunities.

Method Ask yourself Example: AI Emotional Support
1. Similarities What do papers agree on? Several papers study how AI can respond empathetically.
2. Differences How are their approaches different? One focuses on empathy; another on support strategies.
3. Contradictions Where do findings disagree? One study reports benefits; another finds little improvement.
4. Evolution How has research changed over time? Empathetic replies → support strategies → adaptive support.
5. Limitations What are the weaknesses of existing work? Good chatbot replies may not mean better well-being.
6. Missing Factors What important factors were overlooked? Culture, personal values, humor, personality.
7. Missing Contexts Where has the idea not been tested? Different countries, ages, or real-life situations.
8. Integration Can we combine ideas from different papers? Combine emotion detection with cultural adaptation.

Remember: Comparison shows differences. Synthesis explains what those differences mean.

SECTION

Methodology

Now let's look at how we can design a solid methodology for our research.

CONTRIBUTIONS

Different research contributions need different evidence

A good methodology must be reproducible.

Contribution What others learn Example Suitable evidence Need clear IV / DV / Confounding?
Controlled experiment Whether a change causes a difference Do supportive AI replies make students feel more understood than neutral replies? Groups, measured outcomes, fair comparison, statistics Yes: IV = what changes; DV = what you measure.
Design contribution How to design better systems, and why Design an AI tool that helps people understand their emotions Design theory, prototype, user feedback, design lessons Not common: usually an interview and system-level behavioral analysis
Algorithm / model / technique A better way to solve a technical problem Propose new model or new metric for measuring something Strong baselines, ablation, errors, robustness Yes: IV = new method vs baseline or with/without a component; DV = metrics that your method claims to improve
Dataset A resource for future research A dataset of how people express frustration in different cultures Sampling, labeling quality, coverage, documentation Yes: IV = train with vs without the new dataset; DV = model accuracy or other scores.
Qualitative insight How people experience a phenomenon Why students avoid using mental-health apps Interviews, systematic coding, themes, supporting quotes Commonly no: mostly interview or observation or questionnaires.
Conceptual framework A new way to explain or organize a problem A framework of when AI support helps or undermines autonomy Grounding in literature, coherent reasoning, empirical illustration where appropriate Depends: If we want to prove it works, we need IV and DV.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DESIGN

Affective Diary: designing for emotional reflection

Lindström et al. (2006), Affective Diary: Designing for Bodily Expressiveness and Self-Reflection.

Instead of telling people what they feel, the researchers designed a diary that lets people interpret their own experiences through colors, body shapes, and memories from their phone.

Methodology component What the researchers actually did
Research goal Explore how a digital diary can support reflection on feelings and bodily experiences.
Design idea / theory Instead of telling users, "You are sad," show abstract colors and shapes so they can decide what those representations mean.
Participants The researchers involved 12 people from different backgrounds to understand how people express and reflect on their experiences.
Design process They collected people's experiences, explored design ideas, and built a prototype.
What they built A digital diary that combines body-sensor data like heart rate and skin conductance, photos, and messages into visual representations of personal experiences.
Evaluation / evidence They explored how people might use the diary through participant activities, feedback, and hands-on design exploration.
IV / DV / random assignment Not central here. This is exploratory design research.
Contribution to other researchers A concrete design example of how ambiguous, editable representations can invite personal interpretation and reflection.
Limitation Early prototype and exploratory feedback does NOT establish long-term emotion-regulation benefits.

Lesson: A design contribution can be rigorous by explaining the design choices, process, evidence, and transferable lessons—with or without strong IVs.

WORKED EXAMPLE · CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT

Does ChatGPT help people write faster and better?

Noy & Zhang (2023), Experimental Evidence on the Productivity Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence .

The researchers tested whether people using ChatGPT could finish professional writing tasks faster and produce better work than people without ChatGPT.

Methodology component What the researchers actually did
Research question Does access to ChatGPT improve writing speed and quality?
Hypothesis People with ChatGPT will finish faster and produce higher-quality writing.
Independent variable (IV) ChatGPT access: allowed vs. not allowed.
Dependent variables (DV) Writing time and writing quality.
Participants 453 college-educated professionals.
Control group Participants who completed the writing tasks without ChatGPT.
Experimental group Participants who could use ChatGPT.
Random assignment Participants were randomly assigned to ChatGPT or no-ChatGPT conditions.
Procedure Participants completed professional writing tasks. The researchers recorded how long they took and evaluated their writing.
Analysis Compare average writing time and quality between the two conditions.
Results ChatGPT reduced average task time by about 40% and improved quality ratings by about 18%.
Contribution to other researchers Experimental evidence that generative AI can improve performance on certain professional writing tasks.
Limitation Results from short writing tasks do not prove that ChatGPT improves every type of work or long-term writing ability.

Lesson: A controlled experiment changes one main factor (IV), measures its effect (DV), and uses a comparison group to test whether the change makes a difference.

WORKED EXAMPLE · DATASET

EmpatheticDialogues: teaching AI to respond with empathy

Rashkin et al. (ACL 2019), Towards Empathetic Open-domain Conversation Models: A New Benchmark and Dataset .

The researchers created a dataset of conversations about emotional experiences to help AI learn how people respond empathetically.

Methodology component What the researchers actually did
Research question Can conversations about emotions help AI generate more empathetic responses?
Research gap Existing conversation datasets were not specifically designed to teach AI empathy.
Dataset EmpatheticDialogues: approximately 25,000 conversations about emotional situations.
Data collection People described emotional experiences. Other people responded in conversations about those experiences.
Labels Conversations were associated with 32 emotions, such as sadness, excitement, and frustration.
Data quality The researchers used a structured collection process to gather conversations grounded in emotional situations.
Independent variable (IV) In the model comparison: the training approach, including whether the model learned from EmpatheticDialogues.
Dependent variable (DV) Response quality and empathy, measured using automatic evaluation and human judgments.
Baselines Compare models trained using different conversation datasets and approaches.
Evaluation Test the models on held-out conversations and ask human evaluators to judge their responses.
Results Models trained using empathy-focused conversations generally received better human empathy judgments.
Contribution to other researchers A reusable dataset and benchmark for developing and evaluating empathetic conversational AI.
Limitation Better empathy ratings do not prove that a chatbot improves users' mental health in real life.

Lesson: A dataset paper must explain how the data was collected, why it is useful, and how it helps researchers build or evaluate better models. The dataset itself has no IV or DV; experiments evaluating its usefulness can have them.

EXERCISE

Exercise 4:Different research questions need different methods

You have three research ideas about AI and emotional support. How would you study each one?

For each idea, identify the contribution type, propose a methodology, and explain how you would evaluate your work.

Research idea Contribution type Methodology Evaluation
A. Can empathetic AI replies make students feel more understood than neutral replies? ____________ ____________ ____________
B. How can we design an AI tool that helps people reflect on their emotions without telling them what they feel? ____________ ____________ ____________
C. Can we create a dataset that helps AI understand how people from different cultures express emotions? ____________ ____________ ____________

Think: Which studies need IV, DV, and a control group? Which do not? Why?

ANSWER

Answer to Exercise 4

All three studies concern AI and emotions, but they contribute different knowledge. Therefore, they need different methodologies.

Research idea Contribution type Methodology Evaluation
A. Empathetic vs. neutral AI Controlled experiment Randomly assign students to empathetic or neutral AI replies.

IV: Reply style.
DV: Feeling understood (1–7 rating). 		Compare ratings between groups. Keep scenarios and reply lengths similar. Analyze why some replies work better.
B. AI for emotional reflection Design contribution Study relevant theories. Interview users. Design and build a prototype. Refine it using feedback. Conduct user studies. Examine whether the design helps people reflect on their emotions. Derive reusable design principles.
C. Cultural emotion dataset Dataset contribution Collect emotional expressions from different cultures. Create clear labels. Check annotation quality and cultural coverage. Compare AI models trained with vs. without the new dataset. Measure performance across cultures. Analyze errors and biases.

Important: A dataset does not inherently require IV and DV. However, an experiment testing whether the dataset improves AI performance can have them.

Lesson: Choose your methodology based on what new knowledge you want to contribute, not because every paper must follow the same experiment.

SECTION

Writing the paper

Tell a coherent story: question → evidence → insight.

PAPER WRITING

What belongs in each section?

Section What to write Question to answer
Introduction Context, problem, gap, research question, approach, main findings, contributions. Why should anyone care?
Related Work Synthesize closest research; show agreement, disagreement, and gap. What is known and missing?
Methodology Participants/data, IV/DV, controls, procedures, models, evaluation and analysis methods. Could others repeat and trust this study?
Results Report observations, estimates, tables, uncertainty, and outcomes without overclaiming. What happened?
Analysis / Discussion Interpret mechanisms, errors, robustness, alternatives, implications and limitations. Why did it happen and what does it mean?
Conclusion Answer the question, state contributions and limits, identify next steps. What should readers remember?
