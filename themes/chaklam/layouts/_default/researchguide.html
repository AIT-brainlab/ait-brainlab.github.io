WELCOME

Ultimate Guide to Your Research Success

By Chaklam

Research is not just building an application. It is asking a meaningful question, finding convincing evidence, and contributing knowledge that other people find useful.

The research test: What new understanding will other researchers, designers, or practitioners gain from your paper—and why would it matter to them?

HOW TO THINK

Six levels of thinking — same topic, deeper questions

Follow each column downward: the question becomes less about recalling facts and more about understanding causes and making better decisions.

BloomPersonal life: selling more sushiAI that helps people manage emotionsAI-simulated humans in research
Remember
Information		How many sushi boxes did I sell last week?What emotions do people report when using an AI support chatbot?What answers do AI-generated survey participants give?
Understand
Information		Which sushi sells most, and at what times?How does the chatbot respond when users say they feel sad, angry, or anxious?How similar are those answers to responses from real people?
Apply
Knowledge		Can I use sales patterns to adjust how much sushi I prepare each day?Can I apply established emotion-regulation strategies to the chatbot's responses?Can I use AI-generated responses to test a survey before giving it to real participants?
Analyze
Knowledge		Why do some customers buy once but not return: price, taste, freshness, or variety?Why does the same AI response calm one person but upset another?On which questions, groups, or situations do AI-generated answers differ most from real people's answers?
Evaluate
Wisdom		Which change actually increases repeat purchases without reducing quality or profit?When should AI offer advice, ask a question, adapt its tone, or avoid intervening?For which research questions can simulated answers be useful, and when must we ask real people?
Create
Wisdom		How can I design and test a sushi experience that makes customers return regularly without relying on discounts?How can we design and evaluate AI support that adapts to a person's emotions, values, and culture to help them regulate emotions appropriately?How can we design a research method that uses AI simulations for early exploration but checks important findings with real participants?
THINKING TOOLS

Thinking tools

Original question: “Can an AI chatbot help students cope with stress?”

Thinking toolHow the question changesWhy it helps
Challenge assumptionsCan chatbot advice sometimes increase stress instead of reducing it?Challenges the belief that more AI help is always better.
First principlesWhat do stressed students actually need before we decide to use a chatbot?Starts from the human need, not the technology.
Systems thinkingHow do deadlines, sleep, friends, and chatbot use affect stress together?Looks at interacting causes.
Second-order thinkingIf students rely on a chatbot for months, does their ability to cope independently change?Investigates longer-term effects.
InversionWhen might a chatbot make a stressed student feel worse?Identifies risks and failure conditions.
Root-cause analysisIs the main problem a lack of advice, or an overwhelming workload?Investigates the underlying cause.
Thinking by analogyWhat can chatbot designers learn from how peer-support groups listen?Transfers ideas from another domain.
Compare alternativesWhen is a chatbot more useful than journaling or talking with a friend?Defines a meaningful comparison.
Stakeholder perspectivesWould students, counselors, and instructors define “helpful” in the same way?Reveals competing needs and evaluation criteria.

Thinking tools ↑ help us climb toward deeper questions and wiser judgment.

POSSIBLE ANSWERS

How thinking tools improve the career question

Original question: Can AI help students choose a career?

Thinking toolHow the question changesWhy it helps
Challenge assumptionsCould confident AI recommendations stop students from exploring careers they might enjoy?Tests whether more guidance is always beneficial.
First principlesDo students need a job recommendation, or a better understanding of their interests and values?Starts with the underlying need.
Systems thinkingHow do family expectations, financial pressure, grades, and AI recommendations jointly shape choices?Looks at interacting influences.
Second-order thinkingAfter using AI career advice repeatedly, do students become more or less confident deciding independently?Examines longer-term consequences.
InversionWhen might AI career guidance lead students to regret a choice?Finds failure conditions.
Root-cause analysisIs career uncertainty caused by missing information or by conflicting personal goals?Investigates the underlying cause.
Thinking by analogyCould AI borrow the questioning approach used by good career counselors rather than giving instant answers?Transfers a useful approach.
Compare alternativesDo students make better decisions with AI recommendations, self-reflection questions, or human counseling?Identifies meaningful baselines.
Stakeholder perspectivesDo students, parents, and career counselors disagree about what makes a career choice successful?Reveals different definitions of success.

NOVELTY

Novelty is judged by the research community

Novelty is not about what feels new to you. Other researchers decide whether your idea is new relative to published work, interesting to the field, and important enough to change understanding or practice. The only credible starting point is to read closely related papers.

IdeaNovel?Why?
First stress chatbot at my universityNoA new location alone teaches little beyond prior work.
Use a newer LLM for emotion supportNoA model swap alone is not a research insight.
Discover when AI advice makes people feel less in controlPotentiallyA meaningful effect, if not already established.
Generate 10,000 simulated survey respondentsNoMore simulated responses alone do not establish validity.
Identify which questions AI-simulated people answer differently from real peoplePotentiallyCould show when simulations should not be trusted.
Change a dashboard's colorNoAppearance alone rarely yields new understanding.
Find when AI reminders help beginners but distract experienced studentsPotentiallyCould reveal an important boundary condition.

Methodology & evidence

METHODOLOGY

What makes a good methodology?

Example question: Do reflective AI prompts help students regulate emotions better than direct AI advice?

ComponentWhat to specify
Independent variable (IV)Type of AI response: reflective questions vs direct advice.
Dependent variable (DV)Emotion-regulation outcome, e.g., validated self-report measure, plus appropriate behavioral outcomes.
Confounding variablesBaseline distress, prior AI use, expectations, participant characteristics.
Controls and comparisonSame tasks, duration, interface, and access; suitable non-AI or alternative-AI baseline.
RandomizationHow participants are assigned; order counterbalancing if using both conditions.
Participants / datasetsWho is eligible, sample-size rationale, language, recruitment, and exclusion criteria.
Tasks and proceduresExactly what participants see/do, timing, consent, ethics, and safeguards.
Analysis methodsStatistical comparisons and uncertainty; qualitative themes; error categories; subgroup patterns.
ReproducibilityMaterials, prompts, versions, scripts, coding rules, and limitations.
Venue fitCHI: human behavior, design, and experience. ACL: language phenomena, baselines, error and linguistic analysis.

Which analyses answer which questions?

AnalysisPurpose
Comparison / baselineIs it better than a fair alternative?
AblationWhich component causes the improvement?
Error analysisWhere and why does it fail?
Linguistic analysisDo negation, sarcasm, or phrasing affect results?
RobustnessDoes it hold across people, prompts, or datasets?
Qualitative analysisWhat do users experience, and why?
REAL PAPER · CHI

CHI methodology example: CoAuthor

Lee et al. (CHI 2022): CoAuthor: Designing a Human-AI Collaborative Writing Dataset for Exploring Language Model Capabilities.

Study type: Dataset creation and observation of human–AI writing interactions—not a randomized test of a treatment.

Methodology componentHow to read this paper
Research questionHow do people interact with language-model suggestions while writing?
Independent variable (IV)No single manipulated treatment IV; do not invent one.
Dependent variable (DV)No single primary causal DV; the dataset records writing and interaction behaviors.
Participants / dataHuman writing sessions and their interaction traces; check the paper for recruitment and sample details.
Comparison / baselineBehavioral comparisons can be studied in the collected traces; this is not a controlled AI-versus-no-AI experiment.
ControlsInspect the writing interface, task instructions, and collection protocol.
ConfoundersWriting ability, topic, and familiarity with AI can affect observed behavior; observational patterns are not causal proof.
RandomizationDo not assume random treatment assignment; verify actual collection procedures.
Tasks and proceduresParticipants write with AI assistance; interactions are logged for research.
Analysis methodsStudy interaction traces and writing behaviors; distinguish descriptive patterns from causal claims.
ReproducibilityDataset documentation, collection details, and analysis definitions matter.
ContributionA dataset enabling further study of human–AI collaborative writing.
REAL PAPER · ACL

ACL methodology example: EmpatheticDialogues

Rashkin et al. (ACL 2019): Towards Empathetic Open-domain Conversation Models: A New Benchmark and Dataset.

Study type: Dataset and benchmark construction with computational model evaluation; not a randomized clinical intervention.

Methodology componentHow to read this paper
Research questionCan models generate more empathetic responses in conversations about emotional experiences?
Independent variable (IV)In model comparisons, the model or training approach varies; not a human-treatment IV.
Dependent variable (DV)Response-quality outcomes used in automatic and human evaluations.
Participants / dataConversations grounded in emotional situations, collected from crowdworkers.
Comparison / baselineCompare empathetic-response models against relevant conversational baselines.
ControlsUse comparable data splits and evaluation protocols when comparing models.
ConfoundersDataset artifacts, annotation quality, model capacity, and training differences may influence results.
RandomizationTrain/test splits and evaluation sampling matter; not random assignment to mental-health treatment.
Tasks and proceduresCollect emotion-grounded dialogues, train response models, evaluate generated responses.
Analysis methodsAutomatic and human response evaluation; inspect examples and errors to understand limitations.
ReproducibilityDataset, splits, model settings, metrics, and annotation protocol.
ContributionA benchmark and evidence for studying empathetic conversational responses.

Design a study inspired by a real CHI paper

Starting point: CoAuthor (CHI 2022) studies human–AI collaborative writing. Your new question: “Does showing AI writing suggestions before a student drafts an idea reduce the originality of their writing compared with showing suggestions after they draft?”

Imagine university students writing short creative stories. You have access to the same writing interface in both conditions.

Study componentGiven / hintYour decision
Independent variable (IV)Timing of AI suggestionsWhat are the two conditions? __________
Dependent variable (DV)We care about originality.How would you measure it fairly? __________
ControlBoth groups use the same interface.What else must be kept equal? __________
ConfounderSome students are stronger writers.How could you account for this? __________
AssignmentTwo conditionsHow would you assign participants? __________
Analysis methodsCompare originality across conditions.What else would you examine (e.g., edits, interviews)? __________

There is no single perfect answer. Your goal is a fair design that can actually test the question.

PAPER WRITING

What belongs in each section?

SectionWhat to writeQuestion to answer
IntroductionContext, problem, gap, research question, approach, main findings, contributions.Why should anyone care?
Related WorkSynthesize closest research; show agreement, disagreement, and gap.What is known and missing?
MethodologyParticipants/data, IV/DV, controls, procedures, models, evaluation and analysis methods.Could others repeat and trust this study?
ResultsReport observations, estimates, tables, uncertainty, and outcomes without overclaiming.What happened?
Analysis / DiscussionInterpret mechanisms, errors, robustness, alternatives, implications and limitations.Why did it happen and what does it mean?
ConclusionAnswer the question, state contributions and limits, identify next steps.What should readers remember?
