Ultimate Guide to Your Research Success

By Chaklam

Research is not just building an application. It is asking a meaningful question, finding convincing evidence, and teaching others something useful.

The research test: If someone reads your paper but never uses your application, what valuable knowledge will they gain?

HOW TO THINK

Six levels of thinking — the same topic, deeper questions

Read down each column. The subject stays the same; the depth of thinking changes.

BloomPersonal life: healthy dietAI helping people manage emotionsSimulated humans in research
RememberWhat foods are considered healthy?What kinds of support can an AI chatbot offer when someone feels upset?What is an AI-simulated participant?
UnderstandWhy are some foods healthier than others?How might chatting with AI help someone handle difficult feelings?How does an AI produce answers that resemble a person's?
ApplyCan I use nutrition advice to plan my meals?Can I build an AI chat experience that offers that kind of support?Can I use simulated participants to answer a sample survey?
AnalyzeWhy do I struggle to follow my healthy diet on certain days?Why does the same AI support help some people but upset others?When and why do simulated participants answer differently from real people?
EvaluateWhich diet is actually healthy and sustainable for my circumstances?When is AI support appropriate, and when should it direct people toward human help?For which research questions are simulated participants trustworthy enough?
CreateCan I design and test a sustainable eating plan using what I learned?Can I design and test an AI support approach that adapts safely to different needs?Can I develop and test a way to identify when simulations cannot substitute for real participants?
THINKING TOOLS

One weak question, many stronger questions

Original question: “Can we build an AI chatbot to help students manage stress?”

Thinking toolHow the question changesWhy it helps
Challenge assumptionsDoes AI advice always help, or can it make stressed students feel worse?Questions an untested belief.
First principlesWhat do stressed students actually need: advice, reflection, autonomy, or human support?Starts from the underlying need.
Systems thinkingHow do stress, AI use, social support, and help-seeking affect one another over time?Looks at interacting factors.
Second-order thinkingIf students rely on AI every day, what happens to their independent coping skills later?Considers downstream effects.
InversionUnder what conditions could an AI chatbot increase distress?Finds failure modes.
Root-cause analysisAre students struggling because of stress, lack of sleep, deadlines, or something else?Separates symptoms from causes.
Thinking by analogyCould techniques used by human peer-support groups inform how an AI assistant should respond?Transfers a useful insight to a new setting.
Compare alternativesWhen is AI reflection more helpful than journaling or talking with a peer?Identifies a meaningful comparison.

SECTION

Novelty

New to the field. Interesting to others. Important enough to investigate.

NOVELTY

Novelty is judged by the research community

Novelty is not about what feels new to you. Other researchers decide whether your idea is new relative to published work, interesting to the field, and important enough to change understanding or practice. The only credible starting point is to read closely related papers.

IdeaNovel?Why?
First stress chatbot at my universityNoA new location alone teaches little beyond prior work.
Use a newer LLM for emotion supportNoA model swap alone is not a research insight.
Discover when AI advice makes people feel less in controlPotentiallyA meaningful effect, if not already established.
Generate 10,000 simulated survey respondentsNoMore simulated responses alone do not establish validity.
Identify which questions AI-simulated people answer differently from real peoplePotentiallyCould show when simulations should not be trusted.
Change a dashboard's colorNoAppearance alone rarely yields new understanding.
Find when AI reminders help beginners but distract experienced studentsPotentiallyCould reveal an important boundary condition.

SECTION

Methodology & evidence

Make the question testable and the evidence trustworthy.

METHODOLOGY

What makes a good methodology?

Example question: Do reflective AI prompts help students regulate emotions better than direct AI advice?

ComponentWhat to specify
Independent variable (IV)Type of AI response: reflective questions vs direct advice.
Dependent variable (DV)Emotion-regulation outcome, e.g., validated self-report measure, plus appropriate behavioral outcomes.
Confounding variablesBaseline distress, prior AI use, expectations, participant characteristics.
Controls and comparisonSame tasks, duration, interface, and access; suitable non-AI or alternative-AI baseline.
RandomizationHow participants are assigned; order counterbalancing if using both conditions.
Participants / datasetsWho is eligible, sample-size rationale, language, recruitment, and exclusion criteria.
Tasks and proceduresExactly what participants see/do, timing, consent, ethics, and safeguards.
Analysis methodsStatistical comparisons and uncertainty; qualitative themes; error categories; subgroup patterns.
ReproducibilityMaterials, prompts, versions, scripts, coding rules, and limitations.
Venue fitCHI: human behavior, design, and experience. ACL: language phenomena, baselines, error and linguistic analysis.

Which analyses answer which questions?

AnalysisPurpose
Comparison / baselineIs it better than a fair alternative?
AblationWhich component causes the improvement?
Error analysisWhere and why does it fail?
Linguistic analysisDo negation, sarcasm, or phrasing affect results?
RobustnessDoes it hold across people, prompts, or datasets?
Qualitative analysisWhat do users experience, and why?
REAL PAPER · CHI

CHI example: CoAuthor

Lee et al. (CHI 2022), “CoAuthor: Designing a Human-AI Collaborative Writing Dataset for Exploring Language Model Capabilities.”

CHI methodology lens: Who participated? What writing task did they perform? What interaction logs were collected? How were behaviors coded and analyzed? This is a dataset-and-observation study, not necessarily a randomized IV/DV experiment.

This paper introduced a dataset of human–AI collaborative writing interactions. Its research value is not merely a writing tool: it makes it possible to study how people use language-model suggestions during writing.

Ask as a readerLook for
What is the contribution?A dataset and empirical evidence about human–AI writing behavior.
What is the methodology?Collection and analysis of interaction traces from collaborative writing.
What can others learn?Patterns of how people interact with and incorporate AI suggestions.
What is a follow-up question?When does AI assistance increase or reduce originality or ownership?

Read the CHI paper

REAL PAPER · ACL

ACL example: EmpatheticDialogues

Rashkin et al. (ACL 2019), “Towards Empathetic Open-domain Conversation Models: A New Benchmark and Dataset.”

ACL methodology lens: How were emotional situations and conversations collected? How were data split? Which models and baselines were compared? How was response quality evaluated? Look for annotation quality, evaluation design, and reproducibility.

This work introduced the EmpatheticDialogues dataset and benchmark for conversations grounded in emotional situations. Its research contribution goes beyond making a chatbot: it creates a way to study and evaluate empathetic conversational responses.

Ask as a readerLook for
What is the contribution?A dataset, benchmark, and evaluation of empathetic response generation.
What is the methodology?Emotion-situation conversations, model comparisons, and evaluation.
What can others learn?How models perform on a specific, measurable conversational ability.
What is a follow-up question?When do empathetic-sounding replies fail to provide appropriate support?

Read the ACL paper

EXERCISE · METHODOLOGY

Design a small, fair CHI study

Given: We want to know whether a chatbot that asks students questions about their feelings works better than one that gives direct advice. Both use the same interface. We recruit university students for one brief, low-risk session.

ComponentAlready providedYour small decision
Independent variable (IV)Questions vs direct advice—
Dependent variable (DV)How helpful participants rate the interactionWhat simple 1–5 question would you ask?
ControlSame chatbot interface and time limitWhat else should stay the same?
ConfounderPrior experience with chatbotsHow would you measure it?
AssignmentTwo groupsHow would you assign students fairly?
AnalysisCompare helpfulness ratingsWhat would you conclude if the ratings were equal?

Remember: Helpful ratings do not prove better mental health. Sensitive studies need ethics review and appropriate safeguards.

PAPER WRITING

What belongs in each section?

SectionWhat to writeQuestion to answer
IntroductionContext, problem, gap, research question, approach, main findings, contributions.Why should anyone care?
Related WorkSynthesize closest research; show agreement, disagreement, and gap.What is known and missing?
MethodologyParticipants/data, IV/DV, controls, procedures, models, evaluation and analysis methods.Could others repeat and trust this study?
ResultsReport observations, estimates, tables, uncertainty, and outcomes without overclaiming.What happened?
Analysis / DiscussionInterpret mechanisms, errors, robustness, alternatives, implications and limitations.Why did it happen and what does it mean?
ConclusionAnswer the question, state contributions and limits, identify next steps.What should readers remember?
